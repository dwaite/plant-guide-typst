# Rose/Bacopa Planters Setup & Care Notes

Internal planning note for the 2026 rose/bacopa container plantings.

## Scope

This note is the working guide for the Knock Out Rose + White Bacopa containers for
the 2026 season. It consolidates the current container assumptions, history,
material quantities, planting layout, and refresh sequence into one place.

## Volume Planning

Working assumption for planning:

- Use the 17 in interior diameter as the practical planting-space guide
- Treat the earlier 57 qt figure as a rough manufacturer-volume reference, not the
  more useful planting-layout dimension
- These volume estimates apply to the three front garage planters. The south-side
  experimental cement composite planter has a different internal setup because of
  the self-watering insert and added wick pieces.

At roughly 17 in of usable width, these are best treated as one rose plus edge
companions, not as mixed multi-anchor containers.

Recommended fill strategy for these lightweight planters:

- Reserve roughly the bottom third of each planter for non-soil filler
- Keep a fabric separator above the filler so potting mix does not migrate down
- Use potting mix for the upper two-thirds, where roots actually live

Approximate mix needs under that setup:

- Per planter soil volume: about 38-45 qt, depending on actual interior taper and
  how much bottom filler is used
- All three planters: about 115-135 qt of potting mix
- Bag count at 50 qt each: buy 3 bags for a full rebuild

If only doing the standard annual top-third refresh instead of a full rebuild:

- Per planter refresh volume: about 13-15 qt
- All three planters: about 40-45 qt
- Bag count at 50 qt each: 1 bag is enough

If the existing filler is removed and the planters are filled entirely with mix:

- Total mix needed: likely about 150-180 qt depending on the true interior shape
- Bag count at 50 qt each: 4 bags is the safe buy

Practical buy guidance if reusing most of the existing mix:

- If the old mix is still loose and usable after removing the gravel base, plan on
  buying 1-2 bags to top off, refresh, and reset planting height
- If one pot's mix is clearly spent but the others are still decent, 2 bags is the
  most likely working quantity
- Buy 3 bags only if you expect a near-full rebuild of all three upper root zones
- Buy 4 bags only if you decide not to reuse most of the existing mix at all

## Recommended Physical Setup

Do not reuse a thick pea-gravel drainage layer in the bottoms of these planters. It
adds weight without giving a useful drainage advantage in a container this size,
and it makes fall garage moves harder.

Preferred rebuild approach:

1. Confirm each planter has open drainage holes.
2. Place lightweight, non-soil filler in the bottom 3-5 inches of the planter, not
   a huge deep layer.
3. Cover the filler with landscape fabric or similar permeable separator.
4. Fill the upper root zone with fresh potting mix.
5. Work a small amount of elemental sulfur into the refreshed upper mix.
6. Plant the rose slightly high rather than buried low.
7. Add bacopa around the front/outer rim so it can trail over the edge.
8. Finish with a light 1-2 inch mulch layer on the mix surface if desired.

Filler priorities:

- Best overall choice: upside-down empty nursery pots or sturdy plastic cache pots
  that create air space without absorbing water
- Practical working plan for these pots: use upside-down spare nursery pots for the
  main structure, surround/fill around them with broken-up non-biodegradable EPS from
  clean shipping packages, then add a landscape-fabric separator before potting mix
- Good alternative: cut blocks of rigid closed-cell foam
- Acceptable backup: a small amount of non-biodegradable EPS packing material
  contained below a fabric separator
- Avoid: pea gravel, sand, wood chips, compost-only fill, biodegradable packing
  peanuts, or anything that seals over the drainage holes

Why this approach:

- It cuts a meaningful amount of carry weight
- It preserves most of the root-zone depth for the rose
- It is cleaner and more stable long-term than loose filler throughout the bottom
- It makes future rebuilds easier
- It reuses materials already on hand, so there is no strong reason to buy rigid
  foam unless the available EPS is too crumbly or awkward to work with

## Existing Soil Reuse Strategy

Default approach:

- Reuse a meaningful share of the existing potting mix if it is still physically
  sound
- Blend old and new mix rather than trying to rescue exhausted media with lots of
  amendments

What "physically sound" means here:

- The old mix still wets up reasonably well
- It is not sour-smelling or heavily compacted
- It is not collapsing into dense sludge or fine muck
- It does not show obvious salt crusting or severe root congestion throughout

Recommended reuse plan:

1. Pull the rose root ball and set it aside carefully.
2. Remove and discard the pea gravel base.
3. Screen out dead annual roots, woody debris, and any obvious old root mat from the
   saved mix.
4. Keep the best existing mix from the upper and middle root zone.
5. Blend that saved mix with fresh potting mix rather than putting the old mix back
   in unchanged.
6. Reserve the freshest new mix for the upper zone around the crown and for final
   top-off.

Blend target:

- Roughly 50:50 old mix to new mix is a good default
- Lean more heavily toward new mix in the south pot if it has been the one drying
  fastest or degrading the most

Things worth adding at rebuild time:

- A small amount of elemental sulfur in the refreshed upper mix
- Small-pack mycorrhizal inoculant if following the 2026 container-refresh input
- Optional water-storage polymer crystals at label rate

Things not worth doing as the main plan:

- Trying to "fix" spent container mix mainly with fertilizer
- Adding garden soil or clay soil
- Heavy compost additions that collapse the air space in the container
- Large amounts of perlite or bark if the real problem is already excessive dry-down

When to stop reusing and switch to mostly new mix:

- The old mix has become strongly hydrophobic and resists rewetting
- The rose root ball is packed tight enough that you are effectively repotting anyway
- Drainage and wetting are both poor, which usually means the structure is spent
- The old mix volume left after gravel removal is small enough that saving it adds
  effort without much benefit

## Planting Layout

Use one rose per planter as the central anchor plant.

Use White Bacopa 'Snowstorm Giant Snowflake' as the edge spiller:

- Aim for 2 bacopa plants per planter as the default at this size
- A 3-plant bacopa ring is possible if starting with small nursery plants and the
  rose is not oversized, but it will fill fast
- Keep bacopa near the outer edge, not tight against the rose crown
- Set bacopa plugs so the top of the nursery plug is level with or slightly proud
  of the finished container mix. Do not bury the crown or lower stems under extra
  mix.
- Bias placement toward the front and sides so the spill habit is visible from the
  porch approach
- Give the south-end planter the strongest bacopa count or closest monitoring,
  since that pot gets the hottest exposure

Total seasonal bacopa purchase target:

- 6 plants is the clean default buy
- 9 plants is only justified if you want a fuller immediate look and the bacopa
  starts are small

## Redo Sequence

Best timing:

- Rose structural pruning: late March to April
- Container rebuild/refresh: April
- Bacopa planting: after last frost, around May 10 or later

Recommended sequence:

1. Prune each Knock Out Rose back by about one-third to one-half.
2. Remove dead annual residue and any exhausted top-layer potting mix.
3. If the containers still have a heavy gravel layer or otherwise poor internal
   setup, do a full rebuild rather than just a top refresh.
4. Re-establish the bottom filler zone and separator layer if rebuilding.
5. Add fresh potting mix to the root zone.
6. Blend a small amount of elemental sulfur into the refreshed upper mix.
7. Plant or reset the rose at the correct height.
8. Water thoroughly to settle the mix.
9. Add bacopa after frost risk has passed.
10. Apply mulch only after planting is complete.

## Watering and Feeding After Redo

Watering stays manual:

- Check moisture by pressing a finger about 2 inches into the mix
- Water when dry at that depth
- Water thoroughly until excess drains freely
- Do not let the planters stand in water
- Expect the south-end pot to need water sooner than the two morning-sun pots

Ways to stretch watering intervals a bit:

- Add a light 1-2 inch mulch layer on the mix surface after planting; this is the
  first and simplest improvement
- Keep the lighter filler zone shallow so most of the pot still holds real root-zone
  mix and moisture
- Use a potting mix with good water-holding capacity rather than a very barky, fast
  mix; the current Miracle-Gro Organic Choice assumption is reasonable for that goal
- Tuck bacopa close enough to shade the soil surface, but not so close that it crowds
  the rose crown
- Consider mixing in a small amount of water-storage polymer crystals at rebuild time
  if the south pot still dries too fast after the other changes
- Group the pots as tightly as practical without hurting access; clustered pots lose
  less moisture to sun and wind than isolated pots
- Watch whether the south pot is getting reflected heat from nearby hardscape or the
  garage wall; even a small shift in position can help

Strategies to avoid:

- Oversized gravel or rock topdressing that heats up in the sun
- A very deep filler layer that cuts too much soil volume
- Saucers or blocked drainage that leave the rose roots sitting wet
- Driving fertilizer too hard, which can push excess top growth and increase water use

Reservoir and experiment notes:

- The three front rose pots are not built as reservoir planters
- The south-side experimental planter is the self-watering test case: it has an
  AquaPots Lite insert in the bottom plus two added wick pieces
- Treat the south-side planter's moisture pattern as separate from the three front
  planters; the reservoir may extend intervals, but the rose root zone still needs
  finger-checking from the top
- The reservoir is currently being filled twice weekly and is not running out of
  water. That confirms the reservoir is staying supplied, but does not prove the
  upper root zone is moist enough.
- Use the 2-inch soil moisture check to decide when top watering is needed. Use
  reservoir level only to decide when the reservoir needs refilling.
- Improvised buried reservoirs, spikes, or globes are not the main recommendation for
  the standard front setup because they take up root space and often create uneven
  wet/dry behavior
- If extra watering help is still needed in the standard front planters after the
  rebuild, mulch, mix refresh, bacopa shade, and optional polymer crystals are the
  preferred first steps
- Apply fertilizer and iron as soil/top drenches unless future label or setup review
  gives a clear reason to feed through the reservoir

## South-Side Experiment Troubleshooting

If the south-side bacopa looks worse because it was planted too low, correct the
planting height by lifting the bacopa root ball rather than burying the crown from
above.

Best correction while the planting is still new:

1. Water lightly first if the mix is dry, just enough to keep the root ball intact.
2. Use a narrow trowel or fingers to loosen around the bacopa plug.
3. Lift the whole plug/root ball with as little root disturbance as possible.
4. Add fresh potting mix underneath the plug.
5. Reset the bacopa so the plug surface is level with or slightly above the finished
   container mix.
6. Firm gently around the sides, not over the crown.
7. Water from the top to settle the reset plant, then resume separate moisture checks
   for the self-watering experiment.

Avoid simply topdressing over a low bacopa planting. Extra mix over the crown and
lower stems can keep the center too wet and reduce airflow, especially in the
south-side planter where the AquaPots insert and added wick pieces already change
the wet/dry pattern.

Follow-up:

- Recheck the reset bacopa after 3-7 days.
- Recheck the south-side experiment again 7-14 days farther into the experiment,
  comparing bacopa appearance, 2-inch soil moisture, and reservoir refill interval.

Fertilizer approach:

- Bacopa does not get a separate feed program
- Keep rose fertilizer at the lower end so bacopa does not get pushed into leggy
  growth
- Continue the container iron support approach if midsummer chlorosis appears; use
  existing Liquid Ironite first as the provisional iron source

If buying a fresh container fertilizer for this combined rose/bacopa setup, the
current practical preference in the 2026 purchase notes is:

- Jack's Classic Soluble Petunia FeED 20-6-22

Avoid high-phosphorus bloom-booster products.

## Decision Rule: Refresh vs. Full Rebuild

A normal year should only need a top-third refresh.

Choose a full rebuild if any of these are true:

- The planter still has a heavy gravel drainage layer
- The mix has collapsed badly or turned dense/hydrophobic
- Drainage is poor
- The rose root ball has become difficult to re-wet evenly
- Salt buildup or chronic chlorosis suggests the old media is spent

## Winter Carryover

These are long-term rose containers, not disposable seasonal pots.

- Bacopa is replaced every spring
- Roses stay in the containers year to year
- Winter protection remains essential because root systems in containers can freeze
  solid in Zone 5b
- A lighter base setup is part of the winter-survival strategy because the pots need
  to be realistically movable before hard freezes
