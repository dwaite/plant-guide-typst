# Spring/Fall Pruning Cheat Sheet (Zone 5b, Commerce City)

Use this as a quick timing reference. When in doubt, remove only dead/damaged wood.

## Delay These Right Now

- **Lilac shaping/reduction (Miss Kim, Yankee Doodle):** delay until **immediately after bloom** (typically June). Lilacs flower on old wood.
- **Any major evergreen size reduction (juniper, blue spruce, mugo):** delay; only remove dead/damaged tips now.
- **Agastache for pollinator/wildlife value:** if old stems are still standing in very early spring, okay to delay cleanup briefly; do cleanup before strong new growth.

## Do Now (Late Winter to Early Spring)

- **Yankee Doodle lilac branches with withered leaves from last summer:** prune out now as dead/diseased wood.
  - Cut to healthy green wood or branch collar.
  - If disease is suspected, cut 6-8 inches below visible damage.
  - Disinfect pruners between cuts; bag/trash debris.
- **White yarrow:** remove old top growth; cut down to about 1-2 inches above crown/new basal growth.
- **Agastache (Sunset Hyssop):** remove old brown/woody stems; leave crown intact; cut to about 2-4 inches above basal growth.
- **Rocky Mountain penstemon:** remove clearly dead brown stalks and spent flower stems; keep green basal rosette tissue.
- **Autumn Joy sedum:** remove old stalks to near crown before new shoots elongate.
- **Bluebeard:** hard prune in spring to ~4-6 inches (already done correctly).
- **Ornamental grasses (Blue Grama, Switchgrass):** cut to 4-6 inches before new blades push.

## Spring to Early Summer (After Bloom)

- **Miss Kim lilac:** prune right after bloom; remove dead wood, spent flower panicles, and up to ~1/3 of oldest stems if thinning is needed.
- **Yankee Doodle lilac:** same as above; avoid pruning later than early summer to protect next year's buds.

## Fall (What to Leave vs Cut)

- **Leave standing for winter (preferred):**
  - Ornamental grasses
  - Sedum seed heads
  - Most perennial stems (wildlife and crown protection)
- **Do not hard-cut in fall:**
  - Bluebeard (spring cut only)
  - Lilacs (post-bloom only)
  - Penstemon/agastache crowns (avoid exposing crowns before freeze-thaw cycles)
- **Fall cleanup focus instead:**
  - Remove disease-heavy debris
  - Deep water before freeze
  - Mulch tender crowns (agastache, penstemon, bluebeard) 3-4 inches

## Quick Diagnostic Notes

- **Miss Kim pH-lockout signs:** pale yellow new leaves with green veins, short shoots, weak growth despite survival.
- **Miss Kim poor-drainage/root-stress signs:** wilt while soil is wet, generalized yellowing/decline, persistent wet soil 24-48+ hours after irrigation.
