# Property Layout & Plant Locations

This file documents the physical layout of the property and maps each plant to its
location zone. Update when plants are added, moved, or removed.

Source: Tilly landscaping design package (2023_0711_Waite_PACKAGE-REV-3.pdf, April 2023)
and owner description. Confirmed deviations from design are noted in the Design
Deviations section at the bottom.

**Plan orientation note:** In the Tilly design PDF, the bottom of the page is east
(street/front). South is to the left on the plan; north is to the right.

## Lot Overview

- **Shape:** Trapezoidal — significantly wider at the street-facing (east) front, narrowing
  toward the back northwest corner. The south property line is the diagonal edge.
- **Size:** Slightly under a quarter acre (~10,000 sq ft estimated).
- **Street facing:** East. The house is two-story with the front entrance facing east.
- **South boundary:** 4-foot metal fence — provides no meaningful shade to plantings.
- **Wind:** Prevailing winter winds from north and northwest; back of lot (northwest corner)
  is most exposed.

## Design Deviations from Tilly Plan (April 2023)

### 1. Serviceberry → Japanese Maple Substitution (Confirmed)

**Original design:** Two Autumn Brilliance Serviceberry (*Amelanchier × grandiflora*
'Autumn Brilliance') in the south fence line area.

**Actual installation:** One Serviceberry was replaced by the Emperor Japanese Maple
(*Acer palmatum* 'Emperor I'). The Japanese Maple was placed in the west back yard
center rather than along the south fence line. The remaining Serviceberry stayed in the
back yard in a full-afternoon-sun position.

**Implication for the Serviceberry:** The current south-facing, full-afternoon-sun
position is the most stressful sun exposure on the property. The 2–3 × 2 gph emitter
recommendation in the guide reflects this. Original design placement along the south
fence with neighboring shrubs would have offered slightly more microclimate buffering.

**Implication for the Japanese Maple:** The west-center back yard location receives
substantial afternoon sun — more than the Japanese Maple's preference for dappled or
morning-only sun. This is documented in the Japanese Maple plant file and drives the
elevated emitter count (3 × 2 gph), the supplemental heat-wave hand watering note, and
the twice-annual EDDHA iron drench schedule.

### 2. Agastache: 'Blue Fortune' → Sunset Hyssop Substitution (Confirmed)

**Original design:** Agastache 'Blue Fortune' (*A. foeniculum* hybrid) — blue/purple
flowers, moderate water tolerance, Zone 5 reliably hardy.

**Actual installation:** Agastache Sunset Hyssop (*A. rupestris*) — orange flowers,
highly drought-sensitive, best in fast-draining rock soil. Owner identified this
substitution through visual research after the install.

**Current guide is correct** for the installed plant. The substitution was likely made
for aesthetic reasons (the back yard planting scheme runs heavy on white, blue, and
purple; the orange Sunset Hyssop adds contrast). Care implications: Sunset Hyssop is
more drought-sensitive than 'Blue Fortune' and more critical of drainage — the rock bed
placement along the south fence is well-suited to its needs.

### 3. Porch Planting Area (Partial Deviation)

**Original design:** Showed plantings along the porch line using the existing species
(Daylilies, Miss Kims) as placeholders or design elements.

**Actual installation:** Builder-installed Daylilies (near lawn edge) and Miss Kim
Lilacs (behind, against house) were kept as-is. The Mops Mugo Pine and three Dark
Knight Bluebeards shown on the side of the porch in the design do exist there, in
slightly different configuration than drawn.

## Sun Exposure Groups

Grouped by exposure level — useful when writing care text or assessing heat/drought
stress. Per-plant detail is in the Plant Locations table above.

| Exposure | Plants |
|----------|--------|
| **Morning sun only; afternoon shade from house** (east front yard) | Silver Maples, Miss Kim Lilacs, Daylilies, Knock Out Roses, White Bacopa, Candytuft |
| **Mid-morning sun onward; full afternoon sun** (west back yard) | Emperor Japanese Maple, Dwarf Globe Blue Spruce |
| **Full afternoon sun — most stressful exposure** (south back yard) | Autumn Brilliance Serviceberry |
| **Full sun** (all other locations) | Skyline Honeylocust, Spring Snow Crabapple, Wichita Blue Juniper, Mops Mugo Pine, Yankee Doodle Lilac, Peking Cotoneaster, Dark Knight Bluebeard, White Yarrow, Agastache Sunset Hyssop, Rocky Mountain Penstemon, Autumn Joy Sedum, Blonde Ambition Blue Grama, Northwind Switchgrass |

**Key care implications:**
- Japanese Maple and Blue Spruce receive more afternoon sun than either species prefers — elevated heat and drought stress risk; both have pH stress compounding this.
- Serviceberry has the harshest placement on the property — check most frequently during June–August heat waves.
- East front yard plants (including potted) benefit from afternoon shade — more forgiving of missed waterings than back yard plants.
