## Procedures

### SoakORR Lawn Pass + Sprinkler Inspection

- First pass target: lawn only, about 3,000 sq ft.
- Rate: low label rate first, 3 fl oz / 1,000 sq ft, which is 9 fl oz total for
  the lawn.
- Do not include beds or containers in this first pass. Beds, trees, and
  containers can be handled later with hose-end or bucket workflows.
- Apply after 6pm on a Wednesday or Saturday so water-in can happen immediately
  and legally.
- Split the application by sprinkler group if needed: apply front lawn, run front
  zones; apply back lawn, run back zones.
- Water in immediately with about 1/2 inch. Use catch cans if available to learn
  how long each mixed-head zone takes to reach that depth.
- While zones run, watch for pavement/gutter/fence overspray, misting, pooling,
  downhill flow, water running off before soaking in, blocked heads, tilted
  heads, and corners that stay dry while nearby turf is wet.
- Take notes by zone, not by yard-wide impression.

### Micronutrient / EDDHA Catch-Up

- LESCO Micro Mix: apply only to living in-ground trees, shrubs, and perennials
  at the current label rate. Skip the dead Japanese Maple.
- Monterey Sequestar EDDHA: apply to Honeylocust only for the June EDDHA pass.
- Apply EDDHA solo as a soil drench; do not tank-mix it with LESCO Micro Mix or
  herbicides.
- Use the low end of the Monterey label rate for a routine catch-up pass unless
  Honeylocust has severe visible chlorosis.
- Pour slowly around the Honeylocust drip line/root zone and follow with legal
  water-in.
- Do not double-backfill missed EDDHA. One clean June Honeylocust pass is the
  active task.

### Lilac Pruning

- Prune immediately after bloom so next year's buds are not removed later.
- Remove spent flower panicles by cutting just below the old flower cluster,
  above a healthy pair of leaves.
- Remove dead, broken, crossing, or clearly diseased stems.
- Optional thinning: remove a small number of the oldest, thickest canes at the
  base to open the plant. Do not remove more than about one-third of the oldest
  wood in one season.
- Do not shear into a box or hedge shape.

### Dwarf Globe Blue Spruce Mite Check

- Check weekly from late June through August.
- Hold white paper under a branch and tap the branch sharply.
- Look for tiny moving specks on the paper, fine webbing on branch undersides,
  stippled needles, bronzing, or needle drop.
- Check the hotter/sunnier sides first, then the interior.
- Do not spray unless mites or active damage are confirmed. If confirmed, start
  with a strong water spray; escalate to insecticidal soap or horticultural oil
  only within label temperature limits.

### Container Feed / Iron

- Use the actual on-hand container fertilizer label. Current planning notes favor
  Jack's Classic Petunia FeED for the rose/bacopa pots; use the low end of the
  label rate.
- Use Liquid Ironite/container iron only as a container-soil drench, not as an
  in-ground iron substitute.
- Apply as a top soil drench, not through the reservoir/fill tube.
- Do not tank-mix fertilizer and iron unless both labels allow it. If unsure,
  apply in separate legal watering sessions.
- Keep the south experimental planter on separate observation: reservoir level,
  2-inch soil moisture, bacopa appearance, and whether top-watering is still
  needed.
