# Other Planter Planting Concepts

Internal planning note for the remaining non-rose containers on the property.
This is a concept-selection document, not a committed planting roster.

## Locked Goals

- Three back-of-sectional Veradek long boxes: screening first
- One lower patio Veradek long box: more decorative, but still durable in full sun
- One half whiskey barrel: statement ornamental container
- One self-watering raised bed: mixed edible planter

## Shared Constraints

- Zone 5b
- Patio long boxes and whiskey barrel are full-sun, heat-exposed manual-watered
  containers
- The four Veradek long boxes currently contain Miracle-Gro potting mix, while the
  whiskey barrel and raised bed currently contain a heavier leftover 50/50 soil-compost
  blend from the lawn project
- Long boxes should tolerate occasional dry-down better than a color-only annual mix
- The lower patio long box should coordinate with the screening trio without feeling
  identical
- The whiskey barrel stays outdoors year-round, so overwinter survival should not be
  assumed just because it is larger than the other pots
- The raised bed is meant for practical kitchen use, not novelty crops

## Back-Of-Sectional Veradek Long Boxes

Role:

- Visually soften or partially hide the back of the sectional
- Prioritize upright structure over bloom display

### Default Concept: Repeated Shenandoah Screen

Use the three raised-patio long boxes as a repeated, rhythm-driven screen based on
upright switchgrass.

Recommended palette:

- Shenandoah switchgrass as the main structural plant
- Optional low companion at the base if needed: Blonde Ambition Blue Grama or a
  low sedum kept secondary

Why this is the default:

- Keeps the upright switchgrass look while adding stronger red seasonal color
- Feels more distinct from the existing Northwind switchgrass already planted on the property
- Upright habit is the best fit for the screening job
- It keeps the three containers visually coherent
- It fits the perennial-structure bias better than flower-first mixes

Tradeoffs:

- Strongest structure, weakest floral impact
- Still not a guaranteed outdoor-overwinter container plant in this climate

### Alternate Concept: Switchgrass + Bluebeard Screen

Use one upright grass as the vertical element and Dark Knight Bluebeard as the
seasonal softening shrub/perennial component.

Recommended palette:

- Prairie Winds 'Apache Rose' switchgrass
- Dark Knight Bluebeard

Why it works:

- Keeps the needed height
- Adds a more obviously different switchgrass look than simply repeating Northwind
- Adds late-summer bloom and softer mass at sectional height
- Both are already proven in this yard's full-sun palette

Tradeoffs:

- More seasonal than the repeated-Shenandoah concept
- Less crisp screening effect outside peak growth

### Alternate Concept: Xeric Texture Screen

Use lower-water texture plants where the goal is softening the sectional edge more
than fully rising above it.

Recommended palette:

- Blonde Ambition Blue Grama
- Autumn Joy Sedum
- Optional Agastache accent if more seasonal color is wanted

Why it works:

- Best dry-down tolerance of the three screening concepts
- Strong texture, low fuss

Tradeoffs:

- Softening rather than true concealment
- Visually lighter and shorter than the other two concepts

## Lower Patio Veradek Long Box

Role:

- Coordinate with the screening planters
- Be the prettier, more obviously designed long box because it is actually seen

### Default Concept: Decorative Echo Box

Use the same upright backbone as the screening trio but make the composition more
ornamental at eye level.

Recommended palette:

- Shenandoah switchgrass as the backbone
- White Bacopa 'Snowstorm Giant Snowflake' or similar heat-tolerant trailing annual
  for edge softness
- Optional low sedum for a second durable texture

Why this is the default:

- Reads as part of the same family as the back three boxes
- Gives the visible planter a cleaner decorative finish
- Keeps the main structural logic simple across all four boxes

Tradeoffs:

- Relies on a seasonal trailing component for the prettiest finish

### Alternate Concept: Bluebeard Feature Box

Recommended palette:

- Dark Knight Bluebeard as the central show plant
- Autumn Joy Sedum or Blonde Ambition Blue Grama as companions

Why it works:

- More bloom and color than the screening boxes
- Stays within the proven property palette

Tradeoffs:

- Less upright than a switchgrass-led composition
- More of a feature planter than a matched set

### Alternate Concept: Pollinator Color Box

Recommended palette:

- Agastache Sunset Hyssop
- Blonde Ambition Blue Grama
- Optional trailing annual for spill

Why it works:

- Most colorful and wildlife-friendly of the long-box concepts
- Handles full sun well

Tradeoffs:

- More seasonal and less architectural
- Agastache is shorter-lived and less visually tidy

## Whiskey Barrel

Role:

- Statement ornamental in full sun
- Treat as a strong warm-season composition, not as a dependable overwintered
  perennial container

### Default Concept: Bold Summer Statement

Use a dramatic, summer-long focal planting and treat it as ornamental first.

Recommended palette:

- Purple Majesty millet or another bold upright annual grass as the central height
- White Bacopa or calibrachoa as edge softening
- Optional second hot-sun annual accent if more color is wanted

Why this is the default:

- Gives the strongest first-year visual payoff
- Avoids pretending the barrel is a reliable overwinter container just because it is
  larger
- Accepts the barrel's role as a statement piece rather than a durability test

Tradeoffs:

- Most seasonal and least perennial
- Requires accepting replacement as part of the design

### Alternate Concept: Hardy-Leaning Statement

Recommended palette:

- Prairie Winds 'Apache Rose' switchgrass
- Autumn Joy Sedum
- Optional trailing annual edge

Why it works:

- Stronger chance of repeat structure if a component overwinters
- Lower water demand than a lush annual focal planter

Tradeoffs:

- Less dramatic than the annual-led default
- Still should not be described as reliably overwintering outdoors

### Alternate Concept: Pollinator Barrel

Recommended palette:

- Agastache Sunset Hyssop
- Blonde Ambition Blue Grama
- Low sedum or trailing edge annual

Why it works:

- Strong summer color and pollinator activity
- Good fit with the rest of the property's drought-tolerant aesthetic

Tradeoffs:

- Looser, meadow-like look rather than formal statement structure

## Raised Self-Watering Edible Bed

Role:

- Mixed kitchen garden
- Prioritize compact, high-use crops that suit a contained bed

Assumption:

- This planter is against the south side of the house and gets all-day direct sun
- Fruiting crops are a reasonable default here because the self-watering feature and
  sun exposure support them better than a shadier fallback location would

### Default Concept: Kitchen Staples Bed

Recommended palette:

- One determinate or patio tomato
- Basil
- Parsley
- Chives
- Thyme or oregano

Why this is the default:

- High-use cooking crops
- Tomato uses the self-watering feature well if the site gets enough sun
- Herbs fill in around the main fruiting plant without wasting space

Tradeoffs:

- Needs support and regular harvest to stay productive
- Warm-season crops will still need to be chosen for compact size

### Alternate Concept: Pepper-And-Herb Bed

Recommended palette:

- One or two compact peppers
- Basil
- Parsley
- Thyme or oregano

Why it works:

- Slightly more forgiving in constrained space than a tomato-led bed
- Still strongly useful for cooking

Tradeoffs:

- Less total harvest volume than a productive tomato setup

### Alternate Concept: Salad-And-Herb Bed

Recommended palette:

- Cut-and-come lettuce or similar greens
- Arugula or spinach by season
- Parsley
- Chives
- Dill or cilantro by season

Why it works:

- Best fallback if the side-house light is less intense
- Fast harvests and easy reset cycles

Tradeoffs:

- Least summer-showy
- More succession-oriented than the fruiting concepts

## Default Recommendation Set

If only one concept is chosen for each group, use:

- Back-of-sectional long boxes: Repeated Shenandoah Screen
- Lower patio long box: Decorative Echo Box
- Whiskey barrel: Bold Summer Statement
- Raised edible bed: Kitchen Staples Bed, unless the site gets less than about six
  hours of direct sun; then switch to Salad-And-Herb Bed
